Northern Guam is a high, flat plateau of limestone, covered in jungle and edged with cliffs that drop straight into the sea. Much of it is Andersen Air Force Base, closed to visitors, but the rest holds some of the island's finest scenery. This day trip starts and ends in Tumon.

## Early morning

On a Saturday or Sunday, start at the **Dededo flea market** soon after dawn, for fresh fruit, plants and a local breakfast plate. It winds down by mid-morning. See [Markets and shopping](/guides/markets-and-shopping).

Then drive to **Two Lovers Point,** the cliff at the north end of Tumon Bay, while it is still quiet. See [Tumon and Two Lovers Point](/guides/tumon).

## Ritidian

From Dededo, follow Route 3 north and then the narrow road down the cliff to **Ritidian Point,** at the island's northern tip. This is the Guam National Wildlife Refuge: a long white beach, clear water, coastal forest and cliffs, with latte sites and caves with ancient rock paintings. The refuge's visitor center has exhibits about its wildlife and history.

- **Check before you go.** The refuge is usually open five days a week, from Wednesday to Sunday, during the day, and it closes on federal holidays and when the sea is dangerous. Access can also change at short notice, so check the refuge's notices the day before.
- **Swim only on calm days, and only inside the reef.** The water past the reef is deep and the currents are strong. There is no lifeguard.
- **Bring everything you need:** water, food and sunscreen. There are no shops nearby.
- **Follow the posted rules,** and leave shells, plants and animals where they are.

Look out for fanihi, the Mariana fruit bat, and ayuyu, the coconut crab, both protected. Most of Guam's native forest birds were wiped out by the brown tree snake, which arrived by accident after the war. The ko'ko', the Guam rail, survived only in captivity for years; it has since been released on small islands free of snakes, including Cocos Island in the south.

## South Pacific Memorial Park

Back on the plateau, in Yigo, **South Pacific Memorial Park** stands where the Japanese army made its last stand in 1944. Its white memorial tower honors all who died in the Pacific war. It is a quiet place for a short stop. See [World War II sites](/guides/war-history).

## The east coast

Head south on Route 15, along the cliffs of the east coast. Between Yigo and Mangilao, the trail to **Pagat** drops through the forest to an ancient CHamoru village site and a sea cave with a freshwater pool. It is steep, so allow two to three hours and bring water. See [Hiking and waterfalls](/guides/hiking-and-waterfalls).

## Back to Tumon

On the way back, cool off at **Micronesia Mall** in Dededo, the island's largest shopping center, with a food court. If it is a Thursday, stay for the evening night market in Mangilao, beside Santa Teresita Church.

## Good to know

- Fill up on gas and water before you leave the main roads.
- Don't photograph base gates or fences, and stay off roads marked as restricted.
- The roads down to Ritidian and along the cliffs are narrow and winding. Drive slowly, and watch for people walking and cycling.
