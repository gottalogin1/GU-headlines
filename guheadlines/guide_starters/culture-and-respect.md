The CHamoru people have lived in the Mariana Islands for thousands of years. Guam today is also home to large Filipino, Micronesian, Asian and American communities, and to a big US military presence. People are warm and easygoing, and a little respect for local ways goes a long way.

## A few words

CHamoru is one of Guam's two official languages, with English. You will hear it in greetings, songs and place names.

| CHamoru | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Håfa adai | Hello, and welcome |
| Si Yu'os ma'åse' | Thank you |
| Biba! | Hooray, cheers |
| Adios | Goodbye |

You will see the people and the language spelled both CHamoru and Chamorro. CHamoru is the spelling in Guam's official writing system; both are used.

## Respect for elders

Elders hold a special place in island families. You may see younger people greet an elder by taking their hand and raising it to their nose, a custom called nginge'. Visitors aren't expected to do it, but politeness and patience with older people are noticed and appreciated.

## Inafa'maolek

Inafa'maolek is the idea of getting along and looking after one another. It is behind the island's generosity: if you are invited to a family party, bring something to share, expect to be told to eat more, and don't be surprised if you are sent home with a plate of food.

## The jungle and old sites

Many people on Guam believe that the spirits of the ancient CHamoru people, the taotaomo'na, live in the jungle, especially around large banyan trees and latte stones. Residents often ask their permission, out loud, before entering the jungle or picking something. Whatever you believe, follow their lead: stay quiet and respectful, and take nothing away.

Never climb on latte stones or take stones, shells or objects from historic sites. It is disrespectful, and it can be against the law.

## Church and fiestas

Most people on Guam are Catholic, and each village celebrates its patron saint with a fiesta. In churches, dress modestly and keep your voice low. The [events calendar](/events) lists many of the island's events.

## What to wear

Dress is casual, but swimsuits are for the beach and the pool. Put on a shirt and shorts or a cover-up in shops, restaurants and villages.

## The military

The US military has large bases on Guam. They are closed to visitors without a pass. Don't take photos of base gates, and don't fly drones near the bases or the airport.

## Liberation Day

On July 21, Guam marks the day in 1944 when US forces returned and the wartime occupation ended. A parade fills Marine Corps Drive in Hagåtña, and a carnival runs for weeks. For many families it is a day of celebration and of remembering what their parents and grandparents lived through.
