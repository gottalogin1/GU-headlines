Guam is a US territory in the western Pacific, the largest and southernmost of the Mariana Islands. Much of it will feel familiar to Americans, with US dollars, US plugs and English everywhere, but the island has its own language, food and easy pace. This guide covers the practical things to sort out in your first few hours.

## Arriving at the airport

Flights land at Antonio B. Won Pat International Airport (GUM), between Tamuning and Barrigada. Most hotels are in Tumon, about a 10 to 15 minute drive away when traffic is light.

- Many hotels and tour packages include a pickup, so check your booking before you look for a taxi.
- Taxis wait outside the arrivals hall. Ask roughly what the fare will be before you set off.
- Rental car companies have counters at or near the airport. See [Getting around Guam](/guides/getting-around) before you decide.

> Guam follows US entry rules, with a special visa waiver for visitors from some countries. Rules change, so check with US Customs and Border Protection or your airline before you fly.

## Money

Guam uses the US dollar. Cards are taken at hotels, malls, most restaurants and tour companies, and ATMs are easy to find. Carry some cash for small things: fruit stands in the villages, food stalls at the night market, and tips.

## Phones and internet

Guam's area code is 671, and it uses the US country code, +1. Many US mobile plans include Guam at no extra cost, but not all of them do, so check with your carrier before you travel. Local phone companies sell prepaid SIM cards and eSIMs, and hotels, malls and many cafes have Wi-Fi.

## Power, time and tipping

- **Power:** US-style outlets, 120 volts, with two flat pins. Bring an adapter if your plugs are different. Most phone and laptop chargers work on any voltage; check hair dryers and other heaters.
- **Time:** Chamorro Standard Time (ChST), ten hours ahead of UTC, with no daylight saving. Guam is ahead of the US mainland, which is why the island calls itself the place where America's day begins.
- **Tipping:** as on the US mainland. Around 15 to 20 percent at sit-down restaurants (look for a service charge already on the bill), and a few dollars for tour guides, drivers and hotel staff who help with bags.

## Your first evening

Jet lag and the heat catch up with most people, so keep the first evening simple:

1. Walk along Pale San Vitores Road, Tumon's main street, past the hotels, shops and restaurants.
2. Watch the sun go down over Tumon Bay from the beach. The bay faces west, so sunsets are the main event.
3. If it is a Wednesday, go to the night market at the Chamorro Village in Hagåtña for local food and music.

The [events calendar](/events) shows what else is on while you are here.

## Good to know

- The emergency number is **911**, as in the rest of the US.
- People drive on the right.
- The tropical sun is strong all year, even when it is cloudy. Wear sunscreen and drink plenty of water.
- If a storm is on the way, the [weather page](/weather) shows the National Weather Service's warnings for Guam.
