You need a car on Guam. The island is small, about 30 miles from end to end, but the buses don't reach everywhere, taxis are expensive, and the heat makes long walks hard. Most families have one car, and many have two. This guide covers the big decision, shipping or buying, and what to know once you are on the road.

## Ship your car or buy on Guam?

Military orders to Guam often allow you to ship one car at government cost. Check your orders and ask the transportation office.

**Shipping** makes sense for a newer car you plan to keep, and one you own outright or have the lender's permission to ship. The car goes by sea and takes weeks to arrive, so you need a rental or a second car for the first part of your stay.

**Buying on Guam** makes sense for an older car, or one you would sell anyway. Many families buy a used "island car" from someone leaving Guam, and sell it on when they leave. Base notice boards and online groups are full of them at the summer moving season. New cars are sold by dealers on Guam too, with island prices.

## Salt, sun and rust

The salt air, the sun and the rain are hard on cars:

- Wash the car often, including underneath, and wax it.
- Park in the shade or under a carport when you can.
- Look under any used car for rust before you buy, and have a mechanic check it.
- Expect rubber, plastic and paint to age faster than at home.

## Registration and inspection

Cars on Guam are registered with the Government of Guam's motor vehicle office, part of the Department of Revenue and Taxation. A car usually needs a safety inspection, proof of insurance and the title or shipping papers. A car shipped from the mainland must be registered on Guam within the time the rules allow. Bases also have their own registration or pass for driving on base. Ask at check-in what your base needs.

## Your license

A valid US state license lets you drive on Guam, and the rules for military members and their families allow many to keep their home state license. Others need a Guam license after a time. Rules change, so check with the motor vehicle office and your base before your license expires. Visitors from other countries can drive with their home license for a limited time.

## Insurance

Insurance is required. Some mainland insurers cover cars on Guam, and others don't: call yours before you move. Local insurers on Guam sell policies too. Compare prices, and make sure the policy covers typhoon damage if you want it to.

## The rules of the road

- Guam drives on the right, as on the mainland, with US road signs.
- **Speed limits are low,** and police enforce them, especially in school zones and villages.
- **Seat belts and child seats** are required.
- **Don't drink and drive:** checkpoints are common, and the penalties are serious.
- **Phones:** hands-free only.
- **Buses:** stop when a school bus stops with its lights flashing.

## Driving tips

- **Wet roads are slippery,** especially after a dry spell, when oil rises to the surface. Slow down in the first rain.
- **Potholes** appear quickly after heavy rain. Watch the road.
- **Animals:** dogs, chickens and sometimes deer and pigs cross the roads, especially at night.
- **Rush hour** on the main roads, Marine Corps Drive and Route 1 above all, can be slow. Leave early for work and flights.
- **Gas** costs more than on most of the mainland. See the gas prices on this site.
- **After a typhoon,** stay off the roads until officials say they are clear: fallen poles, lines and trees are dangerous.

## When you leave

Plan the sale or shipment of your car early. Cars sell fastest in the summer moving season, when new families arrive. Keep the title, the registration and the service records together for the buyer.
