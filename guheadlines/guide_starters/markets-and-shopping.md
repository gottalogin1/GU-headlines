Guam has no sales tax, so the price on the tag is the price you pay. Visitors from Asia come for the duty-free shopping and US brands, but the island's markets are where you meet its people and taste its food.

## Night markets

- **The Chamorro Village night market, Hagåtña, on Wednesdays.** The island's best known market: rows of stalls grilling barbecue, plates of red rice and kelaguen, fresh coconuts, local crafts, music and often dancing. It gets busy, and parking fills early in the evening. See [A morning in Hagåtña](/guides/hagatna-walking).
- **The Mangilao night market, on Thursdays.** A smaller, more local market beside Santa Teresita Church, on Route 10 in Mangilao, with food stalls and family groups out for the evening.

Bring cash: many stalls don't take cards. Days and times can change around holidays, so check the [events calendar](/events) or ask at your hotel.

## The Dededo flea market

Every Saturday and Sunday morning, farmers and sellers set up in Dededo from around dawn. Come for tropical fruit and vegetables you won't see in the supermarket, plants, homemade snacks, crafts and second-hand finds, and stay for a breakfast plate of eggs, meat and fried rice. It is mostly over by mid-morning. Some tour companies run early shuttles from the Tumon hotels.

## Malls and outlets

- **Micronesia Mall,** in Dededo, is the island's largest shopping center, with department stores, a food court and plenty of air conditioning.
- **Guam Premier Outlets,** in Tamuning, has outlet stores for US brands.
- **Tumon,** along Pale San Vitores Road, has a large duty-free store, luxury boutiques, souvenir shops and convenience stores open late.
- **Agana Shopping Center,** in Hagåtña, is where many residents shop.

## What to bring home

- **Crafts made on Guam:** woven baskets, hats and ornaments of pandanus and coconut leaf, wood carvings, and jewelry such as the sinahi, a crescent-shaped pendant carved from shell. Buy from the maker at a market when you can, and look for labels saying the item was made on Guam.
- **Island flavors:** bottled hot sauces and finadene, the island's soy, vinegar and chili sauce, packets of achote for red rice, coconut candy and other local snacks. See [Local food to try](/guides/local-food).
- **Something to wear:** shirts and caps with CHamoru designs and words, from local brands.

Fresh fruit, plants and seeds may not be allowed into your home country, or into some US states, so check the rules before you pack them.

## Good to know

- Prices at shops are fixed. At the flea market, buying several things from one seller may get you a better price, but haggling hard is not the custom.
- Shops in Tumon stay open into the evening; shops in the villages close earlier.
- Don't buy anything made from coral, turtle shell or other protected wildlife. It is likely to be illegal to take home.
