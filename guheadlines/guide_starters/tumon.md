Tumon curves around Tumon Bay on Guam's west coast. Most of the island's hotels stand along its beach, and Pale San Vitores Road, the street behind them, is lined with shops, restaurants and bars. If you are staying on Guam, you are probably staying here.

## The beach and the lagoon

A reef runs across the mouth of Tumon Bay, so the water between the beach and the reef is shallow and usually calm. The whole bay is a marine preserve: fishing and collecting are limited, so fish swim close to shore and a mask is all you need to see them.

You can walk along the sand in front of the hotels, and public beach parks have parking, shade and restrooms:

- **Ypao Beach Park,** at the southern end of the bay, has lawns, picnic shelters and an easy way into the water. Big events, such as the Guam Micronesia Island Fair, are held here.
- **Matapang Beach Park,** between the hotels, is a small, quiet park with a sandy beach.
- **Gun Beach,** at the northern end past the last hotel, is named after the Japanese gun from the Second World War that still stands at the end of the sand. The reef comes close here, and it is a favorite spot for snorkeling and sunsets. Stay well inside the reef: the current outside it is strong.

Read [Beaches and staying safe in the water](/guides/beaches-and-water-safety) before you swim.

## Two Lovers Point

At the northern end of the bay, a limestone cliff rises high above the sea. This is Puntan Dos Amantes, Two Lovers Point, and the view from the top takes in all of Tumon Bay and the coast to the north.

The island tells a story about it. In Spanish times, a CHamoru father promised his daughter to a Spanish officer. She loved a young CHamoru man instead, and the two ran away together. Chased to the edge of the cliff with nowhere left to go, they tied their long hair together and jumped into the sea, choosing to stay together forever.

The lookout platform at the edge of the cliff charges a small entry fee, and the park around it has gardens, a shop and places to sit. Go in the late afternoon and stay for the sunset.

## Sunsets

Tumon faces west, so the sun sets over the sea. Anywhere on the beach is good. For something different, try Gun Beach, the lawn at Ypao Beach Park or Two Lovers Point. Several hotels put on dinner shows with island dancing on their beachfront in the evening.

## Along Pale San Vitores Road

The road behind the hotels is Tumon's main street, and it is easy to explore on foot: duty-free and luxury shops, souvenir and convenience stores, restaurants from all over Asia and the Pacific, and bars that stay open late. A shuttle bus for visitors runs along the hotel strip and out to the main shopping centers; ask at your hotel.

The road is named after Diego Luis de San Vitores, the Spanish Jesuit who founded Guam's first Catholic mission in 1668. He was killed in Tumon in 1672 by Mata'pang, a CHamoru chief, after whom Matapang Beach Park is named. A church named after San Vitores stands in Tumon.

## Good to know

- Hotel beach chairs and towels are usually for hotel guests only. Bring your own to the beach parks.
- Don't leave bags or valuables in the car at the beach.
- The sun is strong even in the late afternoon. Wear sunscreen and drink water.
