Some of Guam's best moments happen offshore: a pod of dolphins spinning beside the boat, the sun dropping into the sea on an evening cruise, or a day on a tiny island in a turquoise lagoon. Most boat trips leave from marinas on the west coast, in Agat, Piti and Apra Harbor, and many include a pickup from the Tumon hotels.

## Dolphin watching

Spinner dolphins live along Guam's west coast, and they are named for the way they leap and twist in the air. Morning cruises go out to find them, and they are seen on most trips, though never guaranteed. Many cruises add an hour of snorkeling and lunch on board.

Good operators keep their distance and let the dolphins come to the boat. Don't jump in to swim with them: it disturbs them while they rest.

## Sunset and dinner cruises

The west coast faces the sunset, and evening cruises sail out in the late afternoon with dinner and drinks on board, coming back after dark. They are popular with couples and groups, so book ahead.

## Deep-sea fishing

Charter boats leave from Agat Marina and the Hagåtña boat basin for half-day and full-day fishing trips. The deep water close to shore holds mahimahi, wahoo, tuna and blue marlin. Ask the captain before you go whether you can keep some of your catch; many will clean it for you, and some restaurants will cook it.

Fishing from the shore is popular with residents. If you try it, stay out of the marine preserves, where fishing is limited, and stay off the reef edge.

## Cocos Island

Cocos Island is a small, flat island sitting in a wide, shallow lagoon off Merizo, at Guam's southern tip. Boats leave from Merizo for the short ride across. On the island you can spend the day on the beach, snorkel in the lagoon, and rent kayaks and other water sports gear. Check what is open and book the boat ahead, especially at weekends.

Cocos is also a place where the ko'ko', the Guam rail, has been released into the wild again. This flightless bird disappeared from the wild on Guam after the brown tree snake arrived, and Cocos is free of snakes.

Stay inside the lagoon when you swim: the channels out to sea have strong currents. The sun is fierce on the open sand, so bring shade and plenty of water.

## Paddling and water sports

- **Kayaks and stand-up paddleboards:** paddle the calm lagoon in Tumon in the early morning, or a jungle river on a kayak tour in the Talofofo River valley.
- **Jet skis, parasailing and banana boats:** water sports companies in Tumon and Agat run them. They are best on calm days.

## Before you book

- **Choose a licensed operator** with life jackets for everyone, including small children, and ask about the plan if the weather turns.
- **Check the forecast.** The [weather page](/weather) shows the boating forecast, the surf and any small craft advisories. Operators may cancel when the sea is rough, often only a day or two ahead.
- **If you get seasick,** take something for it before you board, sit in the middle of the boat and watch the horizon.
- **Tell the crew** if you can't swim well. They will keep an eye out and give you a float.
