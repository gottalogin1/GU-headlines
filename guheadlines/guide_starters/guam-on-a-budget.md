Guam has a reputation as an expensive island, and hotel rooms, tours and imported goods can cost more than on the mainland. But many of its best things are free: the beaches, the sunsets, the history and the people. With a little planning, a week on Guam can cost much less than the brochures suggest.

## Free beaches and parks

The sea is free, and so are most of the beaches. Tumon Bay can be reached from public paths between the hotels, not only from the hotels themselves. Beach parks around the island have picnic tables and shelters, and the locals use them for barbecues and parties on weekends. Bring your own snorkel and fins, and the reef in Tumon Bay costs nothing.

See [Beaches and staying safe in the water](/guides/beaches-and-water-safety).

## Free history

- The units of the War in the Pacific National Historical Park, such as Asan Beach, the Asan Bay Overlook and Ga'an Point, are free to visit.
- In Hagåtña, the Plaza de España, the cathedral, Latte Stone Park and the old Spanish bridge cost nothing to see.
- The lookouts on the drive around the south are free, and so are the village churches.

See [World War II sites](/guides/war-history) and [A morning in Hagåtña](/guides/hagatna-walking).

## Eat like a local

The best meals on Guam are often the cheapest:

- **The Wednesday night market** at the Chamorro Village in Hagåtña: grilled meat on sticks, red rice and finadene sauce, at market prices.
- **Plate lunches** from small local restaurants and lunch counters: a big plate of meat, rice and a side, enough for two light eaters.
- **Fiestas:** a village fiesta is open to all, and guests are often welcome to eat.
- **Groceries:** a picnic from a supermarket is the cheapest meal by the sea.

See [Local food to try](/guides/local-food) and the [food stories](/category/food).

## Save on getting around

- Walk and use the shuttles in Tumon, and rent a car only for the days outside it.
- Book a car early in busy seasons, and compare the companies at the airport with those in town.
- Check what your own car insurance or credit card covers before you buy the rental company's.
- Fill up away from the hotel area if you can.

See [Getting around Guam](/guides/getting-around).

## Save on hotels

- Hotels a block or two back from the beach, or outside Tumon, cost less, and the beach is still close.
- Prices are higher in the busy weeks around Japanese, Korean and American holidays. Travel outside them if you can.
- A room with a small kitchen, or breakfast included, saves on meals.

## Low-cost outings

- A free hike on a marked trail, with good shoes and plenty of water. Some trails cross private land or need a guide: see [Hiking and waterfalls](/guides/hiking-and-waterfalls).
- Sunset at a beach park, with food from the market.
- A festival or concert: see the [events calendar](/events) for what is on during your stay.

## Where not to save

Don't save on water, sunscreen and a hat, or on a proper life vest and a guide for activities you don't know. And keep some cash: small stalls, fruit stands and fiestas often don't take cards.
