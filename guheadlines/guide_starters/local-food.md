CHamoru food grew from the island's own traditions, mixed over the centuries with Spanish, Filipino, Japanese and American cooking. It is food for sharing: at a village fiesta or a family party, the table is long and nobody leaves hungry. Here is what to look for.

## Red rice

Rice cooked with achote (annatto) seeds, which turn it a deep orange-red, sometimes with onion and bits of bacon. Called hineksa' agaga' in CHamoru, it comes with almost every meal at a gathering.

## Kelaguen

Chopped meat or seafood mixed with lemon juice, onions, hot peppers and often grated coconut. Chicken kelaguen, with the chicken grilled first, is the classic; shrimp, beef and fish versions are common too. It is bright, sour and spicy, and very good with titiyas.

## Finadene'

The island's everyday sauce: soy sauce or vinegar with lemon juice, onions and hot peppers. It is spooned over rice, grilled meat and fish. Every family has its own way of making it.

## Barbecue

Chicken, short ribs and pork ribs, marinated and grilled over charcoal. Barbecue with red rice and finadene' is the taste of a Guam weekend.

## Kådon pika

Chicken stewed in coconut milk with hot peppers: rich, creamy and as hot as the cook likes it.

## Titiyas

A soft flatbread made with flour or corn, often with coconut. It is eaten warm on the side, or used to scoop up kelaguen.

## Empanåda

Guam's empanadas are small, crisp turnovers filled with a smooth, orange mixture of rice and chicken, colored with achote. They are a favorite snack and party food.

## Sweets

- **Latiya:** sponge cake under a thick vanilla custard, dusted with cinnamon.
- **Buñelos aga:** soft fried dough balls made with ripe bananas.

## A word about peppers

Donne' are small local peppers, and they are very hot. If a dish is "a little spicy", it may still be spicier than you expect. Ask, and try a little first.

## Where to find it

- **Local restaurants and plate lunch counters** all over the island serve red rice, barbecue and kelaguen. Many offer a "fiesta plate" with a little of everything.
- **The Chamorro Village night market** in Hagåtña, on Wednesday evenings, has stall after stall of local food. See [A morning in Hagåtña](/guides/hagatna-walking).
- **Village fiestas** celebrate each village's patron saint, with a mass, a procession and a big meal. If a local friend invites you, go.
