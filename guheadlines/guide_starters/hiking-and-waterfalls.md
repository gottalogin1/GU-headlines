Guam has two kinds of country. The south is old volcanic land: red earth, grassy hills, rivers and waterfalls. The north is a raised plateau of limestone, covered in jungle and edged with cliffs and caves. Both have rewarding hikes, most of them short but steep, hot and muddy.

## Before you go

- **Go with someone who knows the way,** at least the first time. Many trails are unmarked, and some cross private land or land where you need permission. The Guam Boonie Stompers, a local hiking club, lead group hikes most Saturdays for a small fee, and visitors are welcome. They post each week's hike and meeting place ahead of time.
- **Start early.** Mornings are cooler, and afternoon showers turn trails to mud.
- **Bring more water than you think,** at least two liters each, plus a hat and sunscreen. Many trails have no shade.
- **Wear shoes you don't mind ruining,** with a good grip. Guam's red clay is slippery when wet. Long pants protect your legs from sword grass, whose edges cut like paper.
- **Tell someone where you are going** and when you will be back. Phones often have no signal in the valleys.
- **Test ropes before you trust them.** Steep parts of some trails have ropes tied by other hikers.

> Leptospirosis, a disease carried by animals, lives in fresh water on Guam, especially in the southern rivers in the rainy season. Don't swim with open cuts, keep your head above the water in rivers and waterfall pools, and see a doctor if you get a fever in the weeks after a hike.

## Mount Lamlam

At about 1,330 feet (406 meters), Mount Lamlam is Guam's highest point. Islanders like to point out that, measured from the floor of the Mariana Trench to the south-west, it would be taller than Mount Everest.

The trail starts from Route 2 in the hills between Agat and Umatac. It climbs steadily over open, grassy ridges with almost no shade, past crosses on the neighboring peak of Mount Jumullong Manglo, where many people walk on Good Friday. From the top, on a clear day, you can see most of the island. Allow two to three hours for the round trip, follow the signs where the path forks, and go early.

## Pagat Cave and Pagat Point

On the east coast, off Route 15 between Mangilao and Yigo, a trail drops about 400 feet through limestone forest to the shore. On the way down it passes the remains of Pagat, an ancient CHamoru village, with latte stones and stone mortars still lying where they were left.

Near the bottom, a side path leads to a cave with a pool of cool fresh water inside. The trail is short but rocky and steep, and the climb back up is the hard part. The sea at the bottom is rough, with waves breaking on sharp rock: look, but don't swim there.

## Waterfalls of the south

- **Talofofo Falls.** The easiest waterfall to see: a private park with paved paths, a cable car and an entry fee. The cave where Shoichi Yokoi, a Japanese soldier, hid until 1972 is on the grounds.
- **Tarzan Falls.** A short walk down a dirt track, with a steep, roped section at the end, to a waterfall and a shallow pool. Very muddy after rain.
- **Sigua Falls.** A longer, muddier hike through grass and marsh in the southern hills, passing an old tank from the war. Best with a guide.

Waterfalls are fullest in the rainy season, from July to November, which is also when trails are muddiest and rivers can rise fast after a storm. Don't cross a river that is running high.

## Easier walks

- **Ritidian,** at the northern tip, has short forest trails inside the wildlife refuge. See [A day in northern Guam](/guides/northern-guam).
- **The Piti Guns trail** climbs through a mahogany forest to three Japanese guns from the war. See [World War II sites](/guides/war-history).
- **The lookouts on Route 2,** above Sella Bay and Cetti Bay, give the view without the climb. See [Driving the southern villages](/guides/south-loop-drive).

## Respect the land

Take your trash home, and leave plants, shells and stones where they are. Many people on Guam ask the taotaomo'na, the spirits of the ancient CHamoru people, for permission before entering the jungle. Read [Local customs and being a good guest](/guides/culture-and-respect) before you go.

Check the forecast and any flood warnings on the [weather page](/weather) the morning of your hike.
