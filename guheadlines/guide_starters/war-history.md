On December 8, 1941, the same day as the attack on Pearl Harbor on the other side of the date line, Japanese planes bombed Guam. Two days later Japanese troops landed, and the island was occupied for two and a half years. CHamoru families lived through forced labor, hunger, internment camps and killings.

On July 21, 1944, US Marines and soldiers landed at Asan and Agat. The fighting lasted for weeks, and thousands died on both sides. Guam still remembers the day American forces returned as Liberation Day.

Many of the sites are part of the War in the Pacific National Historical Park, run by the US National Park Service.

## Start at the visitor center

The park's T. Stell Newman Visitor Center is on Marine Corps Drive, just outside the main gate of Naval Base Guam. Its museum tells the story of the war in the Pacific and on Guam through objects and the voices of people who lived it, and a short film covers the battle. A Japanese midget submarine sits outside. The center is not open every day, so check before you go.

## The landing beaches

- **Asan Beach.** A wide, grassy park beside the sea, where Marines came ashore. Memorials and a few guns stand along the shore. It is a peaceful place for a walk.
- **Asan Bay Overlook.** Up on Nimitz Hill, a lookout over the landing beach. A long memorial wall carries the names of CHamoru people who died or suffered during the occupation and of the American servicemen who died retaking the island.
- **Ga'an Point, Agat.** On the shore of Agat, the southern landing beach, a Japanese gun and concrete bunkers still face the sea.

## The Piti guns

In the hills above Piti, three Japanese coastal guns stand where they were placed before the battle, the only guns in the park still in their original places. They were meant to fire on ships landing at Asan, but they were never used. A trail with steep steps climbs to them through a mahogany forest. Wear good shoes and bring water.

## Remembering the people of Guam

Some of the most important places are memorials to the island's own people:

- **Manenggon,** in Yona, where thousands of CHamoru people were forced to march to camps in the hills in July 1944.
- **Tinta and Faha,** in Merizo, where villagers were killed in the last weeks of the occupation. Memorials in the village remember them.

These are places of mourning for families still living on Guam. Visit quietly, and take nothing away.

## Japanese memorials

- **South Pacific Memorial Park,** in Yigo in the north, stands where the Japanese army made its last stand and had its final command post. A white memorial tower, built in 1970 with help from people on Guam and in Japan, honors all who died in the Pacific war and stands for peace between the two peoples.
- **Yokoi's cave,** at Talofofo Falls park, is named after Shoichi Yokoi, a Japanese soldier who never surrendered. He hid in the jungle nearby until hunters found him in 1972, 28 years after the battle.

## Liberation Day

Every July 21 a parade fills Marine Corps Drive in Hagåtña, and a carnival runs for weeks. See [Fiestas and festivals through the year](/guides/festivals-and-fiestas).

## Good to know

- **Never touch old shells or metal objects** you find in the jungle or on the reef. Unexploded bombs and shells from the war are still found on Guam. Leave them, note the place, and call 911.
- The navy and air force bases are closed to visitors without a pass, and some battle sites are inside them.
- Several sites are close together: Asan Beach, the Asan Bay Overlook and the Piti guns fit easily into one morning. Add Ga'an Point on the way south, and continue with [Driving the southern villages](/guides/south-loop-drive).
