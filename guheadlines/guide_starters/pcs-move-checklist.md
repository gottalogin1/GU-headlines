Orders to Guam mean a move across the Pacific to a small island with its own rhythm. Most families end up loving it: the beaches, the food, the friendly villages and the close military community. But an overseas move takes more planning than a move between states, and Guam's distance makes some things slower and more expensive. This checklist puts the steps in order. Your base, your sponsor and your transportation office have the final word on the rules for your service and your orders.

## Four to six months before

- **Read your orders closely:** command sponsorship for your family, the date you must report, and what is authorized for your household goods and car.
- **Ask for a sponsor** from your new unit if you don't have one. A good sponsor answers questions, sends photos and can help with housing.
- **Start the overseas screening** for family members (medical, dental and, where it applies, special needs). It must be done before family travel is approved, and it can take weeks.
- **Pets:** start now. Guam is free of rabies and has strict rules for bringing animals in, with long waiting times. See [Bringing your pets to Guam](/guides/pets-to-guam).
- **Passports:** get them for everyone, even though Guam is part of the US. Many flights connect through Asia, and you will want them for trips to Japan, Korea, the Philippines and beyond.

## Two to three months before

- **Household goods:** book your shipment with the transportation office. Shipments to Guam go by sea and take weeks to months, so plan what goes in your express or unaccompanied shipment (kitchen basics, bedding, school things, work uniforms) and what you carry in your suitcases.
- **Your car:** decide whether to ship it, sell it or buy on Guam. See [Cars and driving on Guam](/guides/cars-and-driving).
- **Housing:** contact the base housing office about the wait list on base, and learn about the allowances for living off base. See [Finding a home on Guam](/guides/military-housing).
- **Schools and child care:** contact the school liaison officer, and put your children on the child care wait list early. See [Schools and child care for military families](/guides/schools-and-childcare).
- **Medical records and prescriptions:** gather copies, and get enough of each medicine to last the first weeks.

## The last month

- Collect school records, vaccination records, birth and marriage certificates, car titles and registration, and powers of attorney, and carry them with you.
- Forward your mail, and update your address with banks and anyone who sends you packages.
- Check what your phone plan costs on Guam. Some US plans charge it as roaming.
- Pack a "first two weeks" suitcase for each person: clothes for heat and rain, swimwear, sunscreen, and anything you can't do without.

## Your first two weeks on Guam

- **Check in** with your unit and the base, and get your family's ID cards and base access sorted.
- **Temporary lodging:** families usually stay in a hotel or base lodging while they look for a home. Ask about the temporary lodging allowance, and keep every receipt.
- **Get a car:** rent one at first, or buy a used one. Taxis are expensive and the buses don't reach everywhere.
- **Find a home** and sign up for power, water and the internet. New accounts often need a deposit and a few days.
- **Loaner furniture:** ask the base about borrowing furniture while your shipment is at sea.
- **Learn the typhoon basics** before you need them. See [Getting your home ready for typhoon season](/guides/typhoon-ready-home).

## Your first months

- **Move in** when your household goods arrive, and check every box against the inventory. Report damage within the time allowed.
- **Register your car** and get the base decal or pass.
- **Make a typhoon kit** and a family plan.
- **Explore:** a weekend drive around the south, the night market in Hagåtña, a beach every week. See [Things to do on Guam](/guides/things-to-do).
- **Meet people:** your unit's family group, your children's school, a village fiesta. Guam is friendly, and it is a small island: you will keep running into the same people.

## Good to know

- Guam is ten hours ahead of UTC, with no daylight saving time: plan calls home for early morning or evening.
- Packages from the mainland take longer and some sellers don't ship to Guam. Order early for birthdays and holidays.
- Prices for groceries and power are higher than on most of the mainland. The base commissary and exchange help, and so do local markets.
- Be patient. Things on Guam can move slower than you are used to, and a smile goes a long way. See [Local customs and being a good guest](/guides/culture-and-respect).
