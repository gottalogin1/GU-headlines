Guam loves a celebration. Village fiestas, holidays and festivals fill the calendar, and visitors are almost always welcome. A fiesta is one of the best ways to see island life, taste home cooking and feel the warmth Guam is known for.

## Village fiestas

Most people on Guam are Catholic, and each village has a patron saint. Once a year, the village celebrates its saint's feast day:

1. **Nine evenings of prayer,** the novena, lead up to the day.
2. **Mass** is followed by a **procession,** with the statue of the saint carried through the village streets, often with music and singing.
3. **The fiesta** itself: families open their homes, and long tables fill with red rice, barbecue, kelaguen, finadene, fish, desserts and much more.

Some villages also hold a fair or a carnival, with food stalls and music, around their fiesta. There are fiestas all year round, often more than one a month.

## Joining in

- Visitors are welcome at the Mass, the procession and the public parts of the celebration. Dress modestly for church.
- If a family invites you to their table, say yes. Bring something to share, such as drinks or a dessert, accept seconds, and thank your hosts.
- You may be sent home with a plate of food. That is the custom, so take it with thanks.
- Ask at your hotel whether a village fiesta, or a fiesta tour for visitors, is on while you are here.

Read [Local customs and being a good guest](/guides/culture-and-respect) for more about island customs.

## Through the year

| When | What happens |
|---|---|
| March | **CHamoru Month** (Mes CHamoru): a month of events celebrating the CHamoru language, music, dance, crafts and food. The first Monday of March is Guam History and CHamoru Heritage Day, a public holiday. |
| March or April | **Holy Week and Easter.** On Good Friday, many people walk up Mount Jumullong Manglo, in the southern hills, to the crosses at the top. |
| July 21 | **Liberation Day,** marking the day in 1944 when US forces returned and the wartime occupation ended. A parade fills Marine Corps Drive in Hagåtña, and a carnival runs for weeks. |
| Dates vary | **The Guam Micronesia Island Fair,** usually at Ypao Beach Park in Tumon, with dance, crafts, food and music from Guam and the islands of Micronesia. |
| December 8 | **Santa Marian Kamalen** (Our Lady of Camarin), the patron saint of Guam: a public holiday, with a procession through the streets of Hagåtña. |
| December | **Christmas,** with lights on homes and villages, parties and carols. |
| All year | **Village fiestas,** one or more most months. |

## Finding the dates

Dates change from year to year, and some events move at short notice. The [events calendar](/events) on this site lists many of the island's events. Parish bulletins and local radio announce the village fiestas, and your hotel can usually tell you which is next.

## Food at the fiesta

The fiesta table is the best place to try island cooking made at home. See [Local food to try](/guides/local-food) to know what you are eating, and save room for dessert.
