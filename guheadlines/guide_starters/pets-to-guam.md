Bringing a dog or a cat to Guam is possible, and many military families do it, but it takes months of planning. Guam is free of rabies, and the island keeps it that way with strict rules for animals coming in. Pets that arrive without every step done, in the right order and at the right time, can be held in quarantine for a long time, at the owner's cost. Start as soon as you know you are moving.

## Who makes the rules

The rules for animals arriving on Guam come from the Government of Guam's Department of Agriculture, through its animal health office. They change from time to time, so always work from their current rules and forms, not from a friend's move a few years ago. The veterinary clinic on your current base, and the one on Guam, know the process well and can help.

## The steps, in order

The details depend on the animal and where it comes from, but for dogs and cats the process usually looks like this:

1. **A microchip** that meets the international standard, put in before the rabies shots. A shot given before the chip may not count.
2. **Rabies vaccinations,** usually two, given at the right interval and recorded with the microchip number.
3. **A rabies blood test** (often called the FAVN test) at an approved laboratory, after the shots. The result must show enough protection.
4. **A waiting period** after the blood test before the animal may arrive. This is the longest step: plan for months, not weeks.
5. **An import permit** and forms sent to the animal health office before the flight.
6. **A health certificate** from a veterinarian, within a few days before travel, with any treatments the rules ask for.

Pets that arrive before the waiting period ends, or with a missing paper, can be placed in quarantine until the rules are met.

## Timing

Work backward from your report date. Because of the vaccinations, the blood test and the waiting time, many families start six months or more ahead. If your orders come late, ask the animal health office about your choices at once: sometimes a pet can follow later, with a friend or a pet shipping company.

## The flight

- **Few airlines** fly pets to Guam, and their rules change: check whether your pet can fly in the cabin, as checked baggage or only as cargo, and book its place early.
- **Hot months:** some airlines don't carry animals in the hold when it is too hot at any airport on the route.
- **Short-nosed breeds** (such as bulldogs, pugs and Persian cats) are refused by many airlines, because they breathe poorly in the heat and under stress.
- **The crate** must meet the airline's size and design rules. Let your pet get used to it weeks before.
- **Connections:** a route through Hawaii or Asia may add rules of its own. Ask the airline and the transportation office.

## On arrival

Animals are checked at the airport. Bring every paper, in order, in a folder you can hand over: microchip record, vaccinations, the blood test result, the permit and the health certificate.

## Living with a pet on Guam

- **Heat:** walk dogs early in the morning and after sunset, never leave a pet in a car, and give plenty of water and shade.
- **Parasites:** fleas, ticks and heartworm thrive in the tropics. Keep up with prevention all year.
- **Toads:** cane toads are common, and poisonous to dogs that bite or lick them. Ask your vet what to do if it happens.
- **Typhoons:** include your pet in your typhoon plan, with food, water, medicine and a crate.
- **Housing:** check the pet rules of base housing or your lease before you sign. See [Finding a home on Guam](/guides/military-housing).
- **Stray dogs:** Guam has many. Keep your dog on a leash and close by on walks.

## If you can't bring your pet

Some families decide that the move is too hard on an older or nervous animal, and leave it with family or friends. If you are thinking of adopting on Guam, the island's animal shelters and rescue groups have many dogs and cats that need homes.
