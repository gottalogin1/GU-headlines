Guam is about 30 miles long and between 4 and 12 miles wide. You can see most of it in a few days, but sights, beaches and villages are spread out, and there is no train. How you get around depends on how far beyond Tumon you want to go.

## Renting a car

Most visitors who want to explore rent a car for at least a day or two. It is the easiest way to reach the southern villages, the lookouts and the quieter beaches.

- Book ahead around holidays, when cars can run out.
- A valid driver's license from home is usually accepted for a short visit. Ask your rental company what they need, and carry your passport.
- People drive on the right, and distances and speed limits are in miles. Speed limits are low, rarely more than 45 miles an hour.

### The main roads

- **Marine Corps Drive (Route 1)** runs along the middle of the island, from the north through Dededo, Tamuning and Hagåtña to the naval base. Most of the island's traffic uses it.
- **Route 2** follows the west coast south from Agat to Umatac and Merizo.
- **Route 4** runs from Hagåtña down the east side of the island to the southern villages.

Together, Routes 2 and 4 make a loop around the south. See [Driving the southern villages](/guides/south-loop-drive).

### Driving tips

- Roads get slippery when rain starts after a dry spell, and heavy downpours flood low spots quickly. Slow down.
- Watch for stray dogs, and for people walking along village roads at night, where there are often no sidewalks.
- Don't leave bags or valuables in sight in a parked car, especially at beaches and lookouts.
- Gas is sold by the gallon. The home page shows the latest gas prices reported in the news.

## Shuttles and trolleys

Trolley-style shuttle buses run on a timetable between the main hotels in Tumon, the big shopping centers and a few sights. Tickets are sold for single rides or for several days. Ask at your hotel's front desk for the current routes and times.

## Public buses

The Guam Regional Transit Authority runs public buses, but there are few routes and buses don't come often, so most visitors don't rely on them.

## Taxis

Taxis wait at the airport and outside the larger hotels, and hotels can call one for you. Ask about the fare before you start a longer trip. For a full day of sightseeing, a tour or a rental car usually costs less.

## Walking

Tumon is easy to walk, with sidewalks along the main road and paths down to the beach. Outside Tumon, sidewalks are rare and the midday heat is tiring, so walking between villages is not practical.

## Tours

If you would rather not drive, day tours with hotel pickup cover the southern villages, snorkeling trips and dolphin watching. They are also an easy way to hear the island's history from someone who grew up with it.
