Guam's water is warm all year, around 27 to 30 °C (81 to 86 °F), and often clear. Coral reefs ring most of the island, with reef fish, turtles and, a little further out, rays and reef sharks. You can see a lot with only a mask and snorkel, and divers come from across Asia for the wrecks and walls.

## Where to snorkel

- **Tumon Bay.** The lagoon in front of the hotels is a marine preserve, so fish swim close to shore. Try the north end, near Gun Beach, where the reef comes closer. See [Tumon and Two Lovers Point](/guides/tumon).
- **Ypao Beach Park,** at the south end of Tumon Bay: easy entry from the sand and shelters for your things.
- **Piti Bomb Holes,** south of Hagåtña: deep holes in a shallow reef flat, full of fish and coral. Another marine preserve. Go at mid to high tide, when there is enough water over the flat to swim rather than walk. The underwater observatory at Fish Eye Marine Park is at the end of the pier here.
- **Ritidian,** at the northern tip: clear water inside a wildlife refuge. It is open only some days of the week; see [A day in northern Guam](/guides/northern-guam).
- **Cocos Lagoon,** off Merizo in the south: a wide, shallow lagoon reached by boat. See [Boats, dolphins and island trips](/guides/on-the-water).
- **Inarajan pools,** on the east coast: natural saltwater pools at the edge of the reef, for calm days only.

## Diving

Dive shops on Guam offer guided dives, introductory dives for people who have never dived, and full certification courses. Most boat dives leave from marinas on Apra Harbor and in Agat. Some of the island's best known sites:

| Site | What it is | For |
|---|---|---|
| Gun Beach | A shore dive at the north end of Tumon Bay, with turtles, rays and coral | All levels |
| Piti Bomb Holes | A shallow, sheltered reef with plenty of fish | Beginners |
| Blue Hole | A vertical shaft in the reef off Orote Point that opens onto a deep wall | Experienced divers |
| SMS Cormoran and Tokai Maru | Two wrecks lying side by side in Apra Harbor, one from each world war | Experienced divers |

The two wrecks are one of the few places in the world where you can see ships from both world wars on one dive. The Cormoran, a German ship, was sunk by her own crew in Apra Harbor in 1917, when the United States entered the First World War. The Tokai Maru, a Japanese freighter, was sunk by an American submarine in 1943 and came to rest beside her. They lie deep, so dive them with a guide and the right training.

## When to go

Most dive and snorkel sites are on the west coast, which is sheltered from the trade winds. The water there is usually calmest in the dry season, from about December to June. In the rainy season, storms can bring rough seas and cloudy water for a few days at a time. Check the surf, the tides and any warnings on the [weather page](/weather) before you go.

## Look after the reef

- Don't stand on, touch or kick coral. Practice floating still before you swim over shallow reef.
- Give turtles space. Don't chase, touch or ride them.
- Don't feed the fish, and take nothing from the water, alive or dead.
- Guam has five marine preserves (Tumon Bay, Piti Bomb Holes, Sasa Bay, Achang Reef Flat and Pati Point), where fishing and collecting are limited. Spearfishing with scuba gear is against the law everywhere on Guam.
- Wear a rash guard to cut down on sunscreen, and choose a sunscreen sold as reef safe.

## Stay safe

1. **Never snorkel or dive alone.** Most beaches have no lifeguard.
2. **Stay inside the reef** when you snorkel from the shore. The reef edge and the channels through it have strong currents. See [Beaches and staying safe in the water](/guides/beaches-and-water-safety).
3. **Wear fins or reef shoes,** and clean any coral cut well.
4. **Don't fly too soon after diving.** Leave at least a full day between your last dive and your flight home, or follow your dive guide's advice.
