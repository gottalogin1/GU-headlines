Hagåtña, once spelled Agana, is the seat of Guam's government and one of its oldest towns. Spanish missionaries arrived in 1668 and made it the center of the island. Most of the old city was destroyed in the fighting of 1944, but what survived, and what was rebuilt, makes for a rewarding morning on foot.

Go early, before the heat, and park near the plaza.

## Plaza de España

The plaza was the heart of Spanish Hagåtña. The Governor's Palace that stood here was lost in the war; what remains are its arches, walls and the azotea, a raised terrace, along with a little round building known as the Chocolate House. It is a quiet, shady place to start.

## The cathedral

Beside the plaza stands the Dulce Nombre de Maria Cathedral-Basilica. The island's first church was built on this spot in 1669; the present building dates from the 1950s. Visitors are welcome outside of services. Dress modestly and keep your voice down inside.

## The Guam Museum

Across the street, the Guam Museum tells the story of the CHamoru people and the island, from the first settlers to the present. Allow an hour, and check the opening days and times before you go.

## Latte Stone Park

A short way from the plaza, Latte Stone Park displays latte: stone pillars topped with cap stones that once held up the houses of ancient CHamoru villages. These were moved here from an old village site in the south. Latte are a proud symbol of the island; look, but don't climb on them.

## Paseo de Susana and the bay

Down by the water, Paseo de Susana is a park built out into the bay, partly from the rubble of the old city. It has a small copy of the Statue of Liberty and views back to the hills.

## The Chamorro Village

Near the Paseo, the Chamorro Village is a cluster of shops and food stalls. On Wednesday evenings it hosts a night market with local food, crafts and music, one of the best ways to taste the island. Days and times can change, so check before you go, or look for it on the [events calendar](/events).

## Fort Santa Agueda

For a view over the whole town and bay, drive up the hill behind Hagåtña to Fort Santa Agueda, a small Spanish fort with a lookout.

> Combine Hagåtña with lunch nearby and an afternoon at the beach in Tumon, about ten minutes' drive north.
