Most of Guam is ringed by a coral reef. Between the beach and the reef lies a shallow, usually calm lagoon, often only waist or chest deep. At the reef's outer edge, waves break and the water drops away to deep ocean. Knowing where those two meet is the key to enjoying Guam's water safely.

## How the reef works

Waves push water over the reef into the lagoon, and that water has to get back out. It does so through channels, gaps in the reef where the water runs out to sea as a strong current. A channel can look calm, darker and deeper than the water around it, which is why swimmers drift into them. Many of Guam's drownings happen at the reef edge or in these channels.

> Stay inside the reef, in the lagoon. The reef edge, where the waves break, is the most dangerous place on any Guam beach.

## Calm places to start

- **Tumon Bay**, in front of the main hotels: a long sandy beach with a shallow, sheltered lagoon. Good for families and first-time snorkelers.
- **Ypao Beach Park**, at the southern end of Tumon Bay: a public park with picnic shelters and an easy way into the water.
- **Piti**, south of Hagåtña: the Piti Bomb Holes are deep holes in a shallow reef flat, full of fish, and Fish Eye Marine Park has an underwater observatory at the end of a long pier.
- **Ritidian Point**, at the northern tip: white sand and clear water inside a national wildlife refuge. The water past the reef is dangerous, it is a long drive, and the refuge is not open every day, so check before you go and follow the posted rules.

## Rules that keep you safe

1. **Never swim alone.** Most beaches have no lifeguard.
2. **Check the conditions first.** The [weather page](/weather) has the surf, any high surf or rip current warnings, and the tides. When there is a warning, stay out of the water.
3. **If a current pulls you out,** don't swim against it. Swim along the beach until you are out of it, or float and wave for help.
4. **Wear reef shoes or fins.** Coral and rocks cut easily, and cuts in the tropics get infected. Clean any cut well.
5. **Mind the tide.** At low tide the reef flat can be too shallow to swim over; walking on it hurts the coral and your feet.
6. **Look, don't touch.** Don't stand on coral, feed the fish or take shells from the water.

## Marine preserves

Guam has five marine preserves, where fishing and collecting are limited to protect the reef: Tumon Bay, Piti Bomb Holes, Sasa Bay, Achang Reef Flat and Pati Point. They are some of the best places to see fish, so bring a mask and leave everything as you found it.

## Back at the car

Thieves know that beachgoers leave bags in their cars. Take valuables with you or leave them at your hotel, and don't leave anything in sight.
