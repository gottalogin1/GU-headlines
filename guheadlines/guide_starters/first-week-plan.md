A week is the right amount of time for a first visit to Guam. It is enough to see the whole island, from the hotels of Tumon to the quiet villages of the south, without spending every day in a car. This plan keeps the mornings for the sights, when it is cooler and the sea is calmer, and leaves the afternoons for the beach, a nap or the pool. Swap the days around to fit the weather and the tides.

## Day 1: Settle in at Tumon

Most flights land late at night or early in the morning, so keep your first day light. Walk the beach in Tumon Bay, float in the calm water inside the reef and get used to the heat. In the late afternoon, drive or take a taxi up to Two Lovers Point for the view over the bay, then come back down for dinner and the sunset from the sand.

Read [Your first day on Guam](/guides/first-day-on-guam) and [Tumon and Two Lovers Point](/guides/tumon) before you go.

## Day 2: Your first snorkel

Tumon Bay itself is a marine preserve: fish swim around your feet a few steps from the shore. Go early, at high tide, when there is more water over the reef. If you have never snorkeled, a guided tour is a good start, with a short lesson and a life vest. In the afternoon, rest. The sun on Guam is strong even on cloudy days.

See [Snorkeling and diving](/guides/snorkeling-and-diving) and [Beaches and staying safe in the water](/guides/beaches-and-water-safety).

## Day 3: Hagåtña, the capital

Spend the morning in Hagåtña: the Plaza de España, the cathedral, Latte Stone Park and the Guam Museum. It is all close together and easy on foot. If your week includes a Wednesday, come back in the evening for the night market at the Chamorro Village, with grilled meat, red rice, music and crafts. It is the easiest place to taste local food in one go.

See [A morning in Hagåtña](/guides/hagatna-walking) and [Local food to try](/guides/local-food).

## Day 4: The southern villages

Rent a car for the day and drive the loop around the south: the lookouts over the bays, the old Spanish fort above Umatac, the village churches, and the natural pools at Inarajan for a swim. The road is narrow and slow in places, so start early and take the whole day.

See [Driving the southern villages](/guides/south-loop-drive).

## Day 5: World War II history

The War in the Pacific National Historical Park has several sites along the west coast: the beach in Asan where American forces landed in 1944, the overlook above it with the names of those who died, and the guns and caves at Ga'an Point in Agat. The visitor center explains the occupation and the battle. Give it a morning; the afternoon is yours.

See [World War II sites](/guides/war-history).

## Day 6: The wild north

Head north to the wildlife refuge at Ritidian, with a long white beach, clear water and forest trails. It is open only on some days, so check before you go, and bring water and food: there is nothing to buy there. On the way back, stop at the lookouts and the shops of Dededo.

See [A day in northern Guam](/guides/northern-guam).

## Day 7: Your choice

Use the last day for what you liked most, or for something new:

- **On the water:** a dolphin-watching boat trip in the morning, when the dolphins are most often seen. See [Boats, dolphins and island trips](/guides/on-the-water).
- **A hike:** a guided trail to a waterfall. See [Hiking and waterfalls](/guides/hiking-and-waterfalls).
- **Shopping:** the malls and the shops of Tumon. See [Markets and shopping](/guides/markets-and-shopping).
- **A village fiesta,** if one falls on your weekend: everyone is welcome. See [Fiestas and festivals through the year](/guides/festivals-and-fiestas).

## If it rains

Showers on Guam are often short. Keep an indoor plan in your pocket for a wet morning, and look at the [weather page](/weather) each evening to plan the next day. See [What to do on a rainy day](/guides/rainy-day).

## Getting around for the week

You can see Tumon on foot and by shuttle, but the rest of the island is much easier by car. Many visitors rent one for the days outside Tumon only. See [Getting around Guam](/guides/getting-around).
