Guam is an easy island for families: short drives, warm and shallow water, English everywhere and US food on every corner. Most families base themselves in Tumon, close to the beach. This guide covers what children tend to love, and the practical things that make the trip go smoothly.

## Beaches for little ones

- **Tumon Bay** has a wide, shallow lagoon with soft sand and gentle water inside the reef. Small children can paddle and dig while you watch from a few steps away. See [Tumon and Two Lovers Point](/guides/tumon).
- **Ypao Beach Park,** at the south end of the bay, has shade, picnic shelters and lawns to run around on.
- **At low tide,** the water in the lagoon is shallow and warm, and the rocks and sand show crabs, sea cucumbers and small fish. The [weather page](/weather) shows the tides.

Put reef shoes on everyone: coral and rocks cut small feet easily. A rash guard and a hat save a lot of sunscreen, and a life jacket or swim vest helps young swimmers, even in the lagoon. Read [Beaches and staying safe in the water](/guides/beaches-and-water-safety) first.

## Sea life without getting wet

- **Fish Eye Marine Park,** in Piti, has a long pier out over the reef to an underwater observatory, a room below the sea with windows onto the coral and fish.
- **Dolphin watching cruises** go out most mornings, and spinner dolphins are often seen close to the boat. See [Boats, dolphins and island trips](/guides/on-the-water).
- **Snorkeling** in the calm water at Tumon or Ypao is a good first try for children who swim well. See [Snorkeling and diving](/guides/snorkeling-and-diving).

## Pools and water parks

Several of the large Tumon hotels have water parks with slides and lazy rivers, free for their own guests. Some sell day passes to visitors staying elsewhere, so ask before you book a hotel without one.

## Easy outings

- **Two Lovers Point,** for the view from the cliff and the story of the two lovers.
- **The Chamorro Village night market** on Wednesday evenings, for music, dancing and food on sticks. Go early, before the crowds.
- **Talofofo Falls park,** with a cable car down to the waterfall.
- **The War in the Pacific park's visitor center,** with a midget submarine outside and a museum inside. Ask about the Junior Ranger booklet. See [World War II sites](/guides/war-history).
- **The Guam Museum** in Hagåtña, for the island's story.

## Practical tips

- **Heat:** plan outings for the morning and late afternoon, and keep the middle of the day for the pool, a nap or somewhere with air conditioning. Carry water everywhere.
- **Car seats:** children must ride in a car seat or booster seat suited to their age and size. Bring your own, or book one with your rental car ahead of time.
- **Supplies:** supermarkets and stores sell diapers, formula, baby food and children's medicine, though prices are higher than on the US mainland because almost everything is shipped in.
- **Mosquitoes:** use insect repellent, especially at dawn and dusk.
- **Doctors:** Guam Memorial Hospital in Tamuning and Guam Regional Medical City in Dededo have emergency rooms, and there are urgent care clinics and pharmacies around the island. In an emergency, call **911**.
- **Strollers** work on Tumon's sidewalks and in the malls, but a baby carrier is easier at the beach and in the villages.
