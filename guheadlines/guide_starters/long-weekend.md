Many visitors come to Guam for only three or four days, often on a short flight from Japan, Korea, Taiwan or the Philippines. That is enough for a real break if you choose well. The trick is to stay close to Tumon, give one day to the rest of the island, and not try to see everything.

## Day 1: Arrive and slow down

Check in, find the beach and the pool, and eat well. Walk Tumon Bay at sunset. If you arrive early in the day, go up to Two Lovers Point in the late afternoon, when the light over the bay is at its best. See [Tumon and Two Lovers Point](/guides/tumon).

## Day 2: The sea

Spend the morning in the water: snorkel in Tumon Bay at high tide, or join a boat trip to see dolphins and snorkel off the west coast. Rest in the afternoon, then go out for dinner and the shops of Tumon in the evening. See [Snorkeling and diving](/guides/snorkeling-and-diving) and [Boats, dolphins and island trips](/guides/on-the-water).

## Day 3: Around the island

Rent a car or join a half-day or full-day island tour. Start in Hagåtña with the plaza and the cathedral, stop at the war memorials in Asan, then drive south to the lookouts, Umatac and the pools at Inarajan. If it is a Wednesday, end at the night market in Hagåtña. See [Driving the southern villages](/guides/south-loop-drive) and [A morning in Hagåtña](/guides/hagatna-walking).

## Day 4: A last morning

Most flights home leave in the night or early morning, so you often have one more day. Keep it simple: a last swim, a big local breakfast, souvenirs, and a nap before the airport. Hotels can usually keep your bags after check-out.

## For couples

- Watch the sunset from the sand or a beach bar in Tumon, and Two Lovers Point by day.
- Book a sunset cruise or a quiet beach on the west coast.
- Try a modern restaurant that cooks with local food, then a fiesta plate at the night market.

## For families

- Stay on the beach in Tumon, where the water inside the reef is shallow and calm.
- Choose short outings: an aquarium or a water park, the beach parks, a short boat trip.
- Plan the hot middle of the day indoors or in the pool.

See [Guam with kids](/guides/guam-with-kids).

## For first-time divers

Guam is one of the easiest places in the Pacific to try diving: warm water, clear visibility and dive shops used to beginners. A "discover scuba" dive with an instructor takes a morning and needs no license. Don't dive within a day before your flight home: ask the dive shop how long to wait. See [Snorkeling and diving](/guides/snorkeling-and-diving).

## What to skip on a short trip

- The long drive to the north on a day the refuge is closed: check first.
- Too many malls: they are much like malls everywhere.
- A packed schedule. The heat tires people out, and Guam is best at an island pace.
