Rain on Guam usually comes as a heavy shower that passes within an hour, often in the afternoon, and the sun comes back out. In the rainy season, from July to November, it can rain on and off all day. Either way, the island has plenty to do while you wait.

## First, wait it out

A shower is the perfect time for lunch. Find somewhere with a view, try something new (see [Local food to try](/guides/local-food)), and check the radar on your phone. Showers often move through from the east, so the sky may already be clearing behind them.

## Indoors

- **Museums.** The Guam Museum in Hagåtña tells the story of the island and its people, and the War in the Pacific park's visitor center has a museum and a film about the battle of 1944. Both close on some days, so check before you go. See [A morning in Hagåtña](/guides/hagatna-walking) and [World War II sites](/guides/war-history).
- **The underwater observatory** at Fish Eye Marine Park, in Piti. The fish don't mind the rain.
- **Shopping centers.** Micronesia Mall, the outlets in Tamuning and the shops of Tumon are all under cover and air conditioned. See [Markets and shopping](/guides/markets-and-shopping).
- **A movie,** at one of the island's cinemas, which show new US films.
- **A massage or spa,** at many of the hotels and at spas along Pale San Vitores Road.
- **The cathedral** in Hagåtña, quiet and cool, when there is no service.

## In light rain

- **Drive the southern villages.** The hills are greenest in the rain and the views clear quickly. See [Driving the southern villages](/guides/south-loop-drive).
- **See a waterfall at its fullest.** Talofofo Falls park has paved paths, so you can see the falls after rain without a muddy hike.
- **Swim anyway,** in the hotel pool or the lagoon, if there is no thunder. You will be wet either way.

## When to change your plans

- **Lightning:** get out of the water and off the beach at the first thunder.
- **After heavy rain,** skip jungle hikes and river crossings: trails turn to slippery mud, and rivers rise fast. See [Hiking and waterfalls](/guides/hiking-and-waterfalls).
- **Swimming near drains and river mouths:** heavy rain washes dirt and bacteria into the sea. Guam's Environmental Protection Agency tests the water at popular beaches and posts advisories when a beach is unsafe for swimming. Wait a day or two after heavy rain, or swim away from streams and drains.
- **High surf and rip currents:** check the warnings on the [weather page](/weather) before any beach or boat trip.
- **Storms and typhoons:** if a tropical storm or typhoon is coming, follow the advice in [Weather and the best time to visit](/guides/weather-and-when-to-visit), and your hotel's instructions.

## Pack for rain

A light rain jacket or a small umbrella, quick-drying clothes, sandals that can get wet, and a waterproof bag or pouch for your phone are all you need. It stays warm when it rains, so you won't need anything heavy.
