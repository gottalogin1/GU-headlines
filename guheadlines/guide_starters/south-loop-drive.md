Southern Guam is a different island from busy Tumon: green hills, red earth, rivers, small bays and villages where life moves slowly. A loop down the west coast and back up the east takes about two hours of driving without stops, so give it most of a day.

This guide follows Route 1 and Route 2 south along the west coast, then Route 4 back north along the east. You can drive it the other way just as well.

## Before you set off

- Start in the morning; the afternoons often bring rain.
- Fill the tank. There are fewer gas stations in the far south.
- Bring cash for fruit stands and snacks, water, sunscreen and reef shoes.
- These are people's home villages. Drive slowly, park considerately, and keep the noise down near homes and churches.

## The west coast

1. **Asan Bay Overlook.** A short detour up Nimitz Hill leads to a lookout over Asan, one of the beaches where US forces landed in 1944. A memorial wall lists the names of people who died during the war.
2. **Ga'an Point, Agat.** On the shore in Agat, guns and bunkers from the war still stand at another landing beach. Both sites are part of the War in the Pacific National Historical Park, run by the US National Park Service.
3. **Sella Bay and Cetti Bay overlooks.** Past Agat, Route 2 climbs along the coast. Pull over at the lookouts for some of the best views on the island: deep green valleys falling to quiet bays. Mount Lamlam, Guam's highest point, rises above.
4. **Umatac.** Tradition holds that Ferdinand Magellan landed in Umatac Bay in 1521. On the hill above the bay, Fort Nuestra Señora de la Soledad, a Spanish fort, looks out over the village and its church.

## The southern tip

5. **Merizo.** The village has an old bell tower and a Spanish-era rectory near the water. Boats leave from Merizo's pier for Cocos Island and the lagoon.

## The east coast

6. **Inarajan.** Natural saltwater pools sit at the edge of the reef, a favorite swimming spot when the sea is calm. In the village, the Gef Pa'go cultural village shows traditional crafts and cooking, and some old houses still line the streets.
7. **Talofofo.** Talofofo Bay is popular with surfers. Inland, Talofofo Falls park has waterfalls and the cave where Shoichi Yokoi, a Japanese soldier, hid in the jungle until 1972.

From Talofofo, Route 4 takes you north through Yona and back to Hagåtña.

> If the sea is rough, skip the swim. Check the surf and any warnings on the [weather page](/weather) before you go, and read [Beaches and staying safe in the water](/guides/beaches-and-water-safety).
