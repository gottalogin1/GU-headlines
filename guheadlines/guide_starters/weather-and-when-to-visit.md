Guam is tropical and warm every month of the year. Daytime highs are usually in the mid to upper 80s °F (about 30 °C), nights stay in the upper 70s (about 25 °C), and the air is humid. The sea is warm enough to swim in all year. What changes with the seasons is the rain, the wind and the chance of a storm.

## The seasons at a glance

| Months | What to expect |
|---|---|
| January to May | The dry season: less rain, steady breezes from the east, the most comfortable months |
| June | Getting hotter and wetter |
| July to November | The rainy season: heavy showers most days, and the highest chance of typhoons |
| December | Getting drier again |

## The dry season

From about January to May, trade winds blow steadily from the east and keep the heat bearable. Rain still falls, usually in short showers. For beach days, hiking and long drives, these are the easiest months.

## The rainy season

From about July to November it is hotter, stickier and wetter. Most days bring a heavy shower or two, often in the afternoon, with sunshine in between, though some days are grey and rainy throughout. Rain rarely stops a trip: plan outdoor things for the morning and keep the afternoon flexible.

## Typhoon season

Typhoons can reach Guam in any month, but they are most likely from about July to November. Storms are tracked for days before they arrive, so there is time to prepare.

Guam's government uses Conditions of Readiness (COR). The island stays at **COR 4**, the lowest level, all year, as a reminder to be ready. **COR 3, COR 2 and COR 1** mean damaging winds are possible within 48, 24 and 12 hours.

If a storm is coming while you are here:

1. Follow your hotel's instructions. Guam's hotels are built of concrete for typhoons.
2. Keep water, snacks, a flashlight and your phone charged.
3. Call your airline: flights are often cancelled before and after a storm.
4. Check the [weather page](/weather), which shows the National Weather Service's warnings for Guam.

## Sun and heat

The midday sun is strong all year, even when it is cloudy. Wear a hat and sunscreen, drink more water than you think you need, and plan walks and hikes for the early morning or late afternoon. Headache, dizziness and nausea in the heat are signs to get into the shade and cool down.

## The sea

The trade winds make the east side of the island rough for much of the year, while the west side, where Tumon and most hotels are, is usually calmer. Big swells from storms far away can still reach any shore. Before a swim, check the surf and the tides on the [weather page](/weather), and read [Beaches and staying safe in the water](/guides/beaches-and-water-safety).
