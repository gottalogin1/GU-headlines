School is often the first thing parents ask about when orders to Guam arrive. Military families on Guam have several choices: schools run for military children by the Department of Defense, the island's public schools, and a range of private and religious schools. Child care on base fills up fast, so the earlier you plan, the better. This guide explains the choices and the first people to call.

## Start with the school liaison officer

Every installation has a school liaison officer, whose job is to help military families with schools. Contact the one for your new base as soon as you have orders. They know which schools your children can attend, how to enroll, what records to bring, and how to transfer credits and special education plans. They can also put you in touch with parents who have made the same move.

## Department of Defense schools

The Department of Defense Education Activity runs schools on Guam for the children of military members and some other federal families: elementary and middle schools near Andersen Air Force Base and Naval Base Guam, and a high school. Classes follow the same curriculum as other Department of Defense schools around the world, which makes moving in and out easier.

Who can attend depends on your status and the rules of the year. Ask the school liaison officer whether your children are eligible, which school they would attend, and whether school buses serve the area where you plan to live. Where you live can decide where your children go to school, so ask before you sign a lease.

## Guam public schools

The Guam Department of Education runs the island's public schools in villages across Guam. Some families choose them to be part of the community, or because they live far from the base schools. Visit the school, ask about class sizes, buses, the school calendar and what the school offers your child, and talk to parents in the village.

## Private and religious schools

Guam has many private schools, many of them Catholic, and some international and Christian schools. Many have waiting lists and entrance tests, and their school year can differ from the mainland's. Apply early, and ask about fees, uniforms, buses and before- and after-school care.

## Homeschooling

Some military families homeschool on Guam. Check the Government of Guam's rules for registering and reporting, and ask the school liaison officer about groups of homeschooling families and what the base offers them.

## What to bring

Carry these with you, not in your household goods:

- Report cards and transcripts, and recent test results.
- Vaccination records: schools on Guam check them, and missing shots can delay enrollment.
- Birth certificates and your orders.
- Special education plans and evaluations, and medical plans for allergies, asthma or other needs.
- For high school students: course lists and descriptions, to transfer credits.

## Child care

Base child development centers, and family child care homes on base, have long waiting lists, especially for babies and toddlers. Request care through the Defense Department's online child care system (MilitaryChildCare.com) as soon as you have orders: your place in line can start before you arrive. Ask about school-age care and summer camps too.

Off base, Guam has private day care centers and preschools. Visit them, check that they are licensed, and ask other parents.

## Activities and sports

Base youth programs offer sports, camps and clubs. Off base, there is youth soccer, basketball, baseball, swimming, paddling, martial arts and dance across the island, and Guam's schools compete with each other in many sports. Children who join something in their first weeks usually settle in fastest.

## Good to know

- The school year on Guam usually starts earlier than on much of the mainland. Check the calendar of your children's school before you plan your report date.
- Children should drink plenty of water and wear sunscreen at recess and sports.
- School is closed when a typhoon is near. See [Getting your home ready for typhoon season](/guides/typhoon-ready-home).
- For weekends, see [Guam with kids](/guides/guam-with-kids).
