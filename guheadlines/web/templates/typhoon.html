How shelters work on Guam

  • Guam Homeland Security / Office of Civil Defense opens emergency shelters, mostly at public schools, before a storm. Which schools open changes with each storm, so check its newest release before you go.
  • Shelters open hours before the damaging winds. Go early: travel is dangerous once COR 1 is set.
  • You register when you arrive. Bring a photo ID for each adult.
  • Stay in the shelter until the all clear.

What to bring

  • Food and water for about a week, and a can opener
  • Medicines, glasses and medical supplies
  • Bedding: a mat, blanket or sleeping bag, and a pillow
  • Photo ID and important papers in a waterproof bag
  • Flashlight, batteries and a phone charger
  • Clothes, toiletries, and diapers or formula for babies

COR levels: what they mean and what to do

The Governor sets Guam's Condition of Readiness (COR) as a storm comes closer. Damaging winds are winds of 39 mph or more.

  1. COR 4{% if cor.level == 4 %} Now{% endif %}

    {{ cor_levels[4] }}

    • Keep your typhoon kit stocked and your plan up to date.
    • Know where you would go if your home is not safe.
    • Trim trees and fix loose roofing before the season.

  2. COR 3{% if cor.level == 3 %} Now{% endif %}

    {{ cor_levels[3] }}

    • Buy water, food, medicine, fuel and batteries now.
    • Fill the car's tank and get cash.
    • Stay out of the water: the surf builds early.
    • Bring in or tie down anything the wind could throw.

  3. COR 2{% if cor.level == 2 %} Now{% endif %}

    {{ cor_levels[2] }}

    • Put up shutters and board windows.
    • Fill containers and the bathtub with water for washing.
    • Charge phones and power banks; set the fridge to its coldest.
    • If your home is not safe, go to a shelter or a sturdy home now.

  4. COR 1{% if cor.level == 1 %} Now{% endif %}

    {{ cor_levels[1] }}

    • Stay inside, away from windows, in the strongest room.
    • The calm eye is not the end: the wind comes back from the other side.
    • Listen to the radio for the all clear.

After the storm

  • Wait for the all clear before you go out.
  • Stay away from downed power lines, and call GPA about them.
  • Run a generator outside only, far from doors and windows: its fumes can kill.
  • If GWA puts out a boil-water notice, boil tap water until it is lifted.{% if settings.outages_interval_minutes %} See power and water outages.{% endif %}
  • Photograph damage before you clean up, for insurance and aid.
  • Check on neighbors, older people and anyone living alone.
